Research area

Fasting & Longevity

Therapeutic fasting, calorie restriction, and the science of metabolic renewal at Buchinger Wilhelmi Clinics — the world's leading centre for fasting medicine.

I am lead data scientist for the Buchinger Wilhelmi clinic, which specialises in therapeutic fasting. This role supports the scientific documentation of the effects of the Buchinger fasting program, develops new therapeutic strategies, and increases clinical impact through innovation, knowledge exchange, and collaboration.

Overview

Fasting as medicine

Therapeutic fasting at Buchinger Wilhelmi involves medically supervised fasting periods of 5–21 days, during which patients consume only juices, broths, and water. This induces a profound metabolic shift from glucose to fat metabolism, with far-reaching effects on inflammation, gut microbiota, and cellular repair mechanisms.

Our longitudinal clinical studies — spanning thousands of patients — document measurable improvements in metabolic syndrome markers, blood pressure, insulin sensitivity, and quality of life, providing the scientific foundation for what has been practised at this clinic for over a century.

Gut microbiome

How fasting reshapes the gut microbiome

The gut microbiome undergoes dramatic changes during therapeutic fasting. Using shotgun metagenomics and metabolomics, we characterise how the microbial community composition shifts as the gut transitions from a fed to a fasted state, and how these changes persist after refeeding.

These microbiome changes appear to contribute to the sustained health benefits observed in patients after fasting: shifts in short-chain fatty acid producers, reduction of pro-inflammatory bacterial species, and restoration of barrier integrity are among the mechanisms under active investigation.

Metabolic effects

Metabolic normalisation and longevity pathways

Extended fasting activates longevity-associated pathways including autophagy, AMPK signalling, and SIRT1 activation. Our metabolomics studies document the stepwise metabolic transition during fasting — from glycogen depletion through ketogenesis — and correlate these molecular events with patient-reported outcomes.

A key research direction is understanding which patients benefit most, and how to personalise fasting protocols based on baseline microbiome composition, metabolic phenotype, and genetic background.

Clinical translation

From bench to bedside

A central goal of my role at Buchinger Wilhelmi is the translation of basic science insights into improved clinical protocols. This includes the development of validated biomarkers for monitoring fasting efficacy, the design of nutritional refeeding strategies that preserve fasting benefits, and the integration of digital health tools for remote patient monitoring.

Close collaboration between the clinical team, data science, and international academic partners ensures that findings from our unique patient cohort contribute to the broader evidence base for fasting as a therapeutic modality.
